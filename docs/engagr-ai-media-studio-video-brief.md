## 1. The structural insight that drives everything

There are **two fundamentally different things** both called "video" in this system, and conflating them is the trap:

1. **Baked AI video clips** — pixels generated by a model (Veo, Seedance, etc.) or uploaded dealer footage. These are *media assets*. On the timeline they behave like audio clips: positioned, trimmed, sequenced. ffmpeg handles them natively, no browser involved.
2. **Live GSAP compositions** — the Banner Studio lineage: price callouts, offer text, logos, disclaimers, animated lower-thirds, vehicle-spec overlays. These are *rendered*, not baked. They only exist in a browser.

A real dealer video ad is almost always a **composite of both**: an AI/footage background base, GSAP overlays on top (price, finance disclaimer, dealer logo), a music bed, and a voiceover. The render architecture in §4 exists entirely to serve this composite. If we only ever needed baked clips, ffmpeg alone would do it; if we only ever needed GSAP, headless Chrome alone would. We need both, composited, deterministically — that's the whole problem.

## 2. Generation layer (video)

### Roster (Proxied; verify before use)
All current video models are **Proxied** (bare `vendor/model` id, no `@cf/`) — `fetch`/poll, results re-uploaded to R2, billed through **AI Gateway Unified Billing prepaid credits** (same operational gate as the audio Proxied models — a $0 balance fails-safe with `2021 Insufficient balance`; load credits before activation). Candidates from the catalog: Veo 3.1 (+ fast), Seedance 2.0 (+ fast), Hailuo 2.3 (+ fast), Grok Imagine Video, Runway Gen-4.5, Vidu Q3 Pro/Turbo, PixVerse v6. They differ on the axes that actually matter for ads:

- **text-to-video vs image-to-video** — the decisive axis for automotive (see §3). Most support both; confirm per model.
- **clip length** — typically 3–16s. Ad-length output is assembled from multiple clips on the timeline, not generated in one shot.
- **native synchronised audio** — Grok Imagine, Seedance 2.0, and Veo can emit their own audio. This must be reconciled with the timeline's music/VO (see §4.3) or you get doubled audio.
- **resolution / cost tier** — "fast" variants trade fidelity for latency and price; default to fast for drafts, full for final.

### Generation is the expensive layer
Unlike audio (cents per clip), video generation is **dollars per clip** and **seconds-to-minutes of latency**. This reframes the UX: video generation is an async, costed, queued operation with a visible spend implication — not an instant preview. Budget guardrails and per-tenant spend caps belong in the governance pane from day one, not bolted on later. Adopt the cost-UX patterns that work: **charge on commit, not on exploration** (a credit is spent on export/render, not on drafting — AdCreative/Creatify); **explicit cheap-draft vs expensive-final tiers** priced per second/clip and shown at the button (Runway Turbo, Pika); and a **per-tenant wallet with a pre-generation estimate ("≈ X credits ≈ $Y") and a hard cap enforced before enqueue** (the gap HeyGen leaves open). Ties to the AI-Gateway per-tenant tagging in §7. (See competitive-patterns doc.)

## 3. Automotive compliance — the risk audio doesn't have

**This is the section to read twice.** Text-to-video of vehicles is a brand-compliance and legal hazard. A generative model will hallucinate badges, grilles, proportions, trim, and features — producing a "car" that is not the dealer's actual stock and may misrepresent an OEM's product. Given ADME's standing OEM compliance constraints (Toyota especially), shipping AI-hallucinated vehicles into dealer advertising is a non-starter.

Mitigation, in order of preference:

1. **Image-to-video from approved assets.** Animate *real* vehicle photography (OEM-supplied or dealer-shot) — subtle parallax, pan, reveal. The vehicle pixels are real; the model only adds motion. This is the safe default for any shot where the vehicle is the subject. Source these approved assets via **URL / inventory-feed ingest** — point at a dealer site or DMS/inventory feed to pull *real* vehicle imagery + specs (the same ingest auto-builds the brand kit). Grounding generation in actual stock is the practical anti-hallucination foundation (AdCreative/Creatify do URL-ingest today). See competitive-patterns doc.
2. **AI video for non-vehicle content only.** Lifestyle/background b-roll, abstract brand motion, environment — where there's no specific product to misrepresent.
3. **Real vehicle shown via stills + GSAP**, with AI video confined to the background base layer.

Build this as a *policy*, not a guideline: the video-generation step should flag/gate text-to-video of vehicle subjects, and the safe path (image-to-video from an approved asset) should be the path of least resistance in the UI. Treat it as a first-class governance rule alongside the model allowlist. Pair the gate with a **role-gated approval step + generation provenance** (every generation and its prompt retained, à la Runway Sessions) so OEM sign-off has an audit trail — "prove this vehicle wasn't hallucinated."

## 4. Editing & render layer (video)

### 4.1 The AV timeline
Extends the audio timeline to multiple tracks: video track(s), GSAP overlay track(s), music, VO, SFX. The **clock rule is unchanged** — `AudioContext.currentTime` remains master; the GSAP timeline and video playback both slave to it. Video adds the requirement that baked clips seek to frame-accurate positions on scrub; the master clock still arbitrates.

### 4.2 Scene/storyboard data model
Audio timeline state was flat JSON. Video needs structure: **scenes → shots**, each shot carrying its base source (`generated_clip` | `uploaded_footage` | `gsap_composition`), an ordered overlay stack, transitions, and per-source audio handling. Stored in **Neon Postgres** per tenant/project (extends the audio timeline-state table; a JSONB column reached via `server/utils/db.ts`); this richer JSON is the single input to the render. **Forward-compat (design now, build later):** the Phase-1 audio timeline schema must be shaped to grow into scenes→shots without rewriting shipped audio projects — a `schema_version` field + additive columns, audio projects reading as the degenerate "one scene, audio-only tracks" case. Designing this into the audio table up front is far cheaper than migrating it later.

### 4.3 Native-audio reconciliation
Every video clip on the timeline needs a per-clip audio mode: **use source audio / mute / duck-under-VO**. Without it, native-audio models double up against the timeline's music and voiceover. Default to **mute** for generated clips (the timeline owns the soundtrack); expose the override.

### 4.4 Render — the composite pipeline
Do **not** make headless Chrome render everything — putting video decode inside headless Chrome is the fragile, slow part. Instead, split by what each tool is good at:

1. **ffmpeg (Container) composites the baked layer** — sequences video clips and footage, applies transitions, lays the audio bed (music + VO + reconciled clip audio), normalises loudness. Fast, deterministic, no browser.
2. **Headless Chrome (Container) renders *only* the GSAP overlay layer** — drives the seekable GSAP timeline to an exact `time()` per frame at a fixed timestep, captures to a **transparent PNG/WebM sequence** (alpha preserved). This is the only thing that genuinely needs a browser, so it's the only thing that pays the headless cost. **Reuse seed:** these overlays *are* Banner Studio GSAP compositions — `app/composables/useBannerTimeline.ts` is already seekable (`seekTo`), and Banner Studio already renders headless via **Cloudflare Browser Rendering** for image export (`server/api/agency/banner-studio/export-image` + the `banner-rooms` worker). The frame-capture path extends that existing infra rather than starting cold. ⚠️ Per the audio brief §4, the banner timeline clock is **poller/rAF-driven today and must be inverted** so the audio clock arbitrates frame `time()`.
3. **ffmpeg overlays** the rendered GSAP layer onto the composited base and muxes to final MP4 (H.264/AAC, platform profile).

This keeps the fragile/slow headless path scoped to the overlay layer, lets ffmpeg do the heavy media work it's fast at, and stays fully deterministic. Remotion is the reference for the *headless-capture* mechanics (React not GSAP — adapt the frame-stepping pattern, don't adopt the tool); the composite split above is the engagr-specific deviation from a pure-Remotion "browser renders all" approach.

### 4.5 Determinism
Fixed frame rate (25/30fps), fixed timestep capture, GSAP seeked to exact `time()` per frame (seekable timelines make this clean), generated clips at known durations. Same Queue→Container→R2→**Neon**-status async wiring as audio — and that wiring already runs in prod (the `audio-jobs` Worker + `RenderContainer` consuming a Cloudflare Queue). The video render is a new, longer/heavier job type on the same rails, plus the headless-overlay Container. **R2 gotcha (shared):** server-side R2 access via the AWS S3 SDK needs `@smithy/fetch-http-handler` in the Workers runtime (or the native R2 binding) — see audio brief §5.

### 4.6 Distribution — per-platform video profiles
Finished video posts through the **existing Social Suite providers** (`server/utils/social-providers/`) — YouTube (resumable upload, incl. Shorts), TikTok (pull-from-URL), Facebook Reels, Instagram Reels/Stories, LinkedIn — so distribution is a **wiring job, not a new build** (see audio brief §5 "Distribution handoff"). The video render must emit **destination-correct profiles**, not one master:
- **Aspect/format per surface** — 9:16 (Shorts / Reels / TikTok / Stories), 1:1, 16:9 (YouTube standard) — driven by the format the user targets, the same way Banner Studio fans out sizes.
- **Duration caps + codec/bitrate** per each provider's `PlatformCapabilities` (`maxVideoSizeMB`, `supportsReels/Stories`); H.264/AAC platform profiles.
- **LUFS per platform** (§4.4 / `profiles.ts`).
- Render writes the variant to R2 and hands the **R2 key** to the provider (pull-from-URL or resumable upload); the existing scheduler owns timing. **Per-tenant OAuth** activates each platform.

## 5. Cost & latency reality

Video is the expensive product on two axes simultaneously:

- **Generation:** dollars + seconds-to-minutes per clip (Proxied video models). Multiple clips per ad. Per-tenant spend caps required.
- **Render:** headless frame capture is slow — a 30s ad at 30fps is ~900 GSAP frames to seek+capture, plus ffmpeg compositing. Container active-CPU billing helps (scale to zero between jobs), but per-render cost/latency must be measured on a real prototype before the feature is scoped or priced to dealers.

Prototype the headless-overlay render path *first*, in isolation, to get a real cost-per-second-of-output number. That figure decides whether the feature is viable as built or needs a cheaper overlay-render strategy.

The prototype must also settle the **capture mechanism**, because two different things wear the "headless" label here: managed **Cloudflare Browser Rendering** (request-oriented — what Banner Studio's image export uses today) vs **self-managed Puppeteer-in-a-Container** (true per-frame `time()` stepping). ~900 frames for a 30s/30fps ad points hard at the self-managed Container — managed Browser Rendering has session/duration limits that frame-by-frame capture will likely exceed — but measure both. The Banner export gives a working Browser-Rendering reference to start from; the frame-stepping loop is the new part.

## 6. Phasing within video

- **V1 — assembly, no generation.** Timeline assembles uploaded footage + image-to-video of approved stills + GSAP overlays + audio. Compliant by construction (no text-to-video of vehicles), exercises the full composite render. Lowest risk, proves the pipeline.
- **V2 — generation in the loop.** Add text-to-video for non-vehicle b-roll, native-audio reconciliation, spend caps. Compliance gating live.
- **V3 — scale & polish.** Transitions library, templated scene presets per OEM brand kit, batch render across the dealer portfolio.

## 7. Infrastructure alignment (reuse existing conventions, don't reinvent)

Video plugs into patterns the platform already runs in production:

- **Companion Workers, not Pages.** Generation and render are async jobs; the Pages worker entry exports only `fetch`+`scheduled`, so a Pages-side queue consumer never fires. Run them in standalone Workers (the `audio-jobs` model), each consuming its own queue.
- **Queues + DLQ + idempotency.** Dedicated `video-gen` and `video-render` queues, each with a `*-dlq` and `max_retries`→dead-letter (the `music-gen` pattern). **An idempotency key on generation is mandatory** — video is dollars-per-clip, so a redelivered/duplicate message must never re-bill; the audio brief's failed-retry/idempotency logic carries over directly.
- **R2 via the native binding inside Workers** (`env.BUCKET.put/get`, as the music worker does) — sidesteps the `unenv: https.request` failure entirely. Reserve the AWS S3 SDK (+ `FetchHttpHandler`) for the Pages app.
- **Per-tenant cost is first-class, not bolted on.** Route Proxied video-model calls through **AI Gateway with per-tenant tagging** so spend is attributable per dealer; enforce caps by **checking accumulated spend before enqueueing** a generation job, and surface estimated spend in the UI as a costed, confirmed action. (Implements the §2/§5/§8 spend-cap requirement on real infra.)
- **Pure, tested render math.** The ffmpeg composite-command builder and the frame-step math are **pure, unit-tested functions** (mirroring `render.ts`); the Container is a thin port kept in sync. §4.5 determinism is then verifiable without spinning a Container.
- **Neon migrations.** Schema ships as additive migrations in `server/database/migrations/` (`IF NOT EXISTS` guards), applied as part of the change — not hand-run.
- **Governance reuses the LLM-role infra.** The §3 compliance gate, model allowlist, and spend caps are new capability buckets on the existing allowlist + RBAC + audit-log + super-admin-redaction pattern — not a parallel governance system.
- **Containers scale to zero.** Reuse the `RenderContainer` shape (instance tier sized to the job, `sleepAfter` to scale to zero between renders); video render is heavier, so confirm the tier on the §5 prototype.

## 8. Open decisions / risks

- **Compliance gating UX.** How hard to gate text-to-video of vehicle subjects — soft warning vs hard block? Needs an ADME/OEM-policy call, not a technical one. Recommend hard block at V1/V2.
- **Headless render cost.** The gating unknown for the whole video phase (§5). Prototype before committing scope.
- **Overlay render strategy.** PNG sequence vs transparent WebM vs direct ffmpeg-piped frames — decide on the prototype based on measured throughput.
- **Per-tenant spend caps.** Required before generation ships. Where they live (governance pane) is known; the limits and billing model are a business decision.
- **Brand kits.** Templated per-OEM scene/overlay presets (logos, fonts, disclaimer placement, colour) would make V3 scale — worth designing the data model for early even if built late.
